# FB CRM — roast verdict (council parts 3–5 + judgment)

Companion to `2026-08-18-fb-crm-roast.md`, which holds the Contrarian and
Expansionist reports. Hermes did not contribute: the bridge round failed on a
Hermes-side `ImportError` in its own tool registry, so no independent audit was
returned.

---

## The Logician (first principles) — score 4/10

**Stance:** the prospecting instinct is sound and time-honored; the pricing
promise is arithmetically fragile, the funnel is ~25 real leads deep, and the
CRM solves a problem he does not yet have.

1. **The "net your full ask" promise breaks on first contact with a negotiating
   buyer.** It closes only at exactly full asking: sell at 1.05X, commission
   0.05X, seller nets X. Boats that already sat unsold at X do not sell at ask.
   At a typical ~5% negotiation off the relisted price (0.9975X), the seller
   nets ~0.9475X — **less than their original FSBO ask**. Either Connor eats the
   gap (commission → 0), the seller eats it (promise broken at the closing
   table, the worst possible moment), or the deal dies.
2. **5% total is half the 10% norm — sounds like an edge, shrinks the buyer
   pool.** Co-broking agents earn 2.5% versus 5% elsewhere and steer buyers
   away. The mechanism funding the trick suppresses the demand needed to sell
   5% *above* a price the market already rejected. The two halves fight.
3. **Funnel math rounds to less than one close.** 97 leads → ~25 true FSBOs
   after the 75% brokered rate → 5–10% cold-DM listing conversion → 1–2
   listings → half sell = **0.5–1 close**. At 5% of a ~$300K median that is
   $15K gross before the house split. 52 of 97 are Alaska while he sits in
   Seattle — showings, surveys and sea trials are a logistics tax on thin
   commission.
4. **The CRM is displacement activity.** A cadence engine, scraper, queue UI and
   digest built to manage a pipeline containing zero live conversations, after
   five DMs. The binding constraint is outbound volume and offer quality, not
   follow-up orchestration.
5. **Arbitrage against himself.** The boat is publicly visible at X; he relists
   at 1.05X. Diligent buyers find the same hull 5% cheaper direct. The model
   requires the seller to pull the Marketplace listing and stay dark.

**Must hear:** decide *before* the next conversation who eats the gap when a
buyer offers under ask, because "you net your full ask" is a promise the
arithmetic cannot keep.

## The Researcher (evidence) — 4/10 as specced, 6.5/10 reshaped

**Stance:** FSBO → brokerage is real and industry-validated; the channel
(Marketplace DMs via scraping on a personal account) and the 5% economics are
where the real world says no.

1. **The winning pitch is "frustrated FSBO", and there is a hard structural
   wedge: private sellers *cannot* list on YachtWorld — it is broker-only.**
   "I get you on YachtWorld" is real and verifiable. No evidence of brokers
   systematically sourcing from Marketplace; the 6-of-8 brokered result suggests
   the $150K+ tier there is mostly brokers cross-posting.
2. **10% with a ~50/50 co-broke split is confirmed as the norm** (YachtWorld,
   YATCO, Windward). At 5% total, other brokers are incentivised to show every
   boat except his.
3. **The Messenger-scraping extension is a genuine account-loss risk and got
   worse in 2025** — ML detection upgraded sharply; extensions simulating input
   trigger hard bans. Passive listing scraping is materially lower-risk than
   automated DMs; that is the line.
4. **Purpose-built tooling is cheap** — YachtCloser, BoatWizard/Boats Group
   cover listing and closing. Marketplace prospecting is genuinely novel; the
   CRM plumbing around it is solved and low-cost.
5. **The long-tail thesis is validated by real data.** Used boats averaged
   **139 days on market in 2025, up 16 YoY**; new+used 209 days; sailboat median
   time-to-sale 172 days. A seller who says no in week 2 is a different person
   at day 120 with moorage, insurance and winter haul-out accruing.

**Must hear:** the durable asset is the follow-up system aimed at *stale* FSBO
listings (60+ days, sub-$500K, genuinely unbrokered), pitched as "you can't get
on YachtWorld without me", at a commission the co-broke network will show.

## The Buyer (the seller being pitched) — score 3/10

Role-played as the owner of the 1962 Broward 64', $500K, Olympia, 6 weeks FSBO.

1. **"I may have a buyer interested" is the oldest two-step in the book.**
   "*May*" means you don't have anybody. A real one says "I have a client
   looking for a classic Broward in the 60–70ft range — can he see her
   Saturday?"
2. **The 5%-over arithmetic is insulting, not clever.** She hasn't moved at
   $500K in six weeks and the plan is to ask $525K. Buyers who already saw her
   watch the price go *up* the day a broker takes over, and know whose pocket
   the $25K goes into.
3. **"I listed her myself specifically so I wouldn't pay a broker."** The pitch
   is "pay a broker, but let's pretend the buyer's paying it."
4. **The photography offer is the only thing that landed.** Three photos of a
   1962 Broward makes her look like a project boat — "the lowballers are pricing
   the photos, not the boat."
5. **Nothing in the message was about the boat.** "Solid boat" is what you say
   about a Bayliner.

**Would sign today for:** a named, pre-qualified buyer with a booked viewing,
commission contingent on that buyer closing; or a *60-day* exclusive with the
full photo/video package delivered whether she sells or not.
**Instant block:** one fake-urgency follow-up.

# THE VERDICT: RESHAPE
**Confidence:** high

**The call in one line:** the sourcing engine is genuinely valuable and worth
keeping, but the commission structure at the centre of the pitch is
arithmetically broken and must be replaced before another terms message goes out.

**Why:** four of five council members, reasoning independently, hit the same
defect — 5%/5% only delivers "you net your full ask" if the boat sells at exactly
the inflated price, which a boat that already failed at the lower price will not
do. The Expansionist's 8/10 is not a disagreement about that; it argues the
*machine* is worth more than the pitch it currently serves. Both are true, which
is why this is a reshape and not a kill.

**Biggest risk:** the pricing promise detonating at the closing table — the
seller discovers they net less than their original ask at the moment trust
matters most. Second: one Facebook restriction taking out the scraper, the DMs
and the Meta ad account together, since all three sit on one identity.

**Biggest upside:** the brokered half of the board is an expired-listing farm and
a co-broke rolodex, and 139-day average days-on-market makes the long-tail
follow-up thesis evidence-backed rather than hopeful.

**Money read:** at a corrected 10% with a 5% co-broke, a ~$300K median boat is
~$30K gross, ~$15K after co-broke, less the JBY house split. First dollar is
realistically 6–9 months out. Honest expected value of the current 97-lead sweep
is **0.5–1 close** — the pipeline needs widening by recency and geography far
more than it needs software.

**The cheapest 48-hour test:** take the ~25 genuinely-FSBO, sub-$500K,
60-day-old listings already in the database and send 20 openers **manually**,
with the pitch rewritten to: no phantom buyer, no 5%-over trick, lead with
"private sellers can't list on YachtWorld — I can", and offer the photo package.
Pre-register the threshold: **fewer than 3 private-seller conversations = the
channel is dead**, and no further CRM work happens.

**The reshape, specifically:**
1. **Replace 5%/5% with 10% total / 5% co-broke** — or 8% with a full 4% to the
   buyer's side. Never a total that starves co-broke.
2. **Delete the "list 5% higher, you net your full ask" promise.** Replace the
   wedge with YachtWorld access, which is structurally true.
3. **Cut "I may have a buyer interested"** from the live script unless a named
   buyer exists.
4. **Re-target: stale, sub-$500K, genuinely unbrokered, near Seattle.**
5. **Keep the follow-up tracker; drop the Messenger scraping.**
6. **Turn `broker_dead` into a 120/210-day expired-listing re-fire.**
